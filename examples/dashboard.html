    SMALL RULES. MEANINGFUL BOUNDARIES.
    More than “message received.”
    01
    The right sender.
    Services authenticate and agree on a contract. A service with no permission cannot negotiate its way in.
    02
    The right data.
    Schema and sensitive-field rules are checked before delivery. Violations can be quarantined with redacted evidence.
    03
    A clear outcome.
    Accepted, duplicated or denied. Follow the broker’s decision and the receiver’s acknowledgement in the audit trail.
    

    
    A SIMPLE IDEA, MADE EXPLICIT.
    Your subject. 
    Your rules.
    A subject is a named destination for messages. Give it a policy; PigeonMQ checks every message against it. Open a step to see the real call and what it refers to.
    1. 1Define the policyWho can send and receive, which schema applies, and what data is allowed.›
      A policy is one JSON file per subject. Its name is the identifier every later step refers to: the contract request asks for it, each message carries it, and every audit record cites the policy it was checked against.
      Lives in
      policies/subjects/payments.authorize.json
      Defines
      name · intents · policy.publish / receive / ack · data.forbiddenFields · delivery.idempotency
      Referenced by
      step 2 as subjects: ["payments.authorize"]; every audit record as policyId
       payments.authorize policy excerpt
      // policies/subjects/payments.authorize.json
{
  "name": "payments.authorize",
  "version": "v1",
  "intents": ["authorize_payment"],
  "policy": {
    "publish": [{ "effect": "allow", "principals": [".../checkout-api"], "intents": ["authorize_payment"], "regions": ["uk", "eu"] }],
    "receive": [{ "effect": "allow", "principals": [".../gateway-adapter"] }]
  },
  "data": { "classification": "pci", "forbiddenFields": ["card.pan"] },
  "delivery": { "idempotency": { "required": true } }
}
    2. 2Negotiate a contractThe broker grants a service the operations its identity is allowed to perform.›
      The sender authenticates and asks for the subject by name. The broker compiles that policy for this identity into a short-lived contract listing only the operations it may perform. Permissions never travel inside the message.
      Call
      POST /v1/contracts with Authorization: Bearer …
      Refers to
      the policy name from step 1
      Produces
      contract.id, the identity it was issued to, permitted operations, policyId, expiresAt

    3. 3Send with confidencePublish under that contract. Inspect the actual outcome and its evidence.›
      Every publish names the subject and carries the contract id in a header. The broker validates the contract, checks the data rules and records a decision. The receiver’s acknowledgement lands in the same audit trail, keyed by the message id.
      Call
      POST /v1/messages with X-Pigeon-Contract: contract_1
      Refers to
      subject from step 1 and the contract id from step 2
      Produces
      message.id; audit records publish.accepted → delivery.dispatched → delivery.acked, each carrying messageId, contractId and policyId
